Given a point $t$ and two function $f$ and $g$ 
- $f(t)$ represents **the signal** (or the information)
- $g(t)$ represents **a filter**
	- a way to change the signal $f(t)$

Convolution is the operation $$(f \ast g)(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} f(\tau) g(t-\tau) \;d\tau$$
In particular we are summing
- every values of $f$ around instant $t$
- each one with a specific weight $g(t-\tau)$

$g$ is also called **weights mask**
- decides how much the past counts compared to the present.


---
Imagine that:
- $f(t)$ is a melody played over time
- $g(t)$ is how a room responds (the echo).
	- how much echo the room adds after a sound on instant $t$

The convolution $$f \ast g$$
- the melody you actually hear in the room,
	- because at every moment the room “adds” a little sound from previous moments (the echo).
- it's the sum of every sound heard before
	- each mini-echo produced by the room
	- that accumulates and overlaps over time

The perceived sound is a weighted mean of the past sounds
- the most recent ones weights more
	- strong echo
- the far ones weights less
	- weak echo
