In standard linear regression, we aim to find weight $w$ that minimize the loss, as sum of squared errors between predictions and true values. $$Loss(w) = \sum_{p=1}^l (y_{p} - x_{p}^T w)^2$$ 
**RIdge regression** modifies this objective by
- adding a penalty on the size of the coefficients $$Loss(w) = \sum_{p=1}^l (y_{p} - x_{p}^T w)^2 + \lambda \lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert^2,\quad \lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert^2 = \sum w_{j}^2,\quad \lambda \geq 0 $$ 
- penalizing models with high values of $\lvert w \rvert$
- favoring **sparse models** 
	- using less terms due to weights $w_{j} = 0$ (or close to it)
	- less complex model
- the $\lambda$ is a regularization hyper-parameter
	- small positive value
	- chosen by the model selection phase

---

Ridge regression is a special case of [[202510051240|Tikhonov regularization]].
