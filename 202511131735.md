We know that with regularization our loss is $$Loss(w) = \underbrace{\sum_{p} (d_{p} - o(x_{p}))^2}_{\text{data term}} + \underbrace{\lambda \lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert^2}_{\text{penalty term}}$$

- low $\lambda$
	- complex model
	- less regularized solutions
- high $\lambda$
	- less complex model
	- more regularized solutions

## Varying the lambda
On 25 data  sets from the sinusoidal
### High lambda
![[Pasted image 20251113174014.png]]
- High $\lambda$, over regularized model
	- Penalty term less dependent from the data
	- too rigid (simple) model
- we got the effect of 
	- high bias, low variance
### Medium lambda
![[Pasted image 20251113174329.png]]
- Decreasing $\lambda$
	- Penalty term more dependent from the data
- we got the effect of 
	- low(er) bias, high(er) variance

### Low lambda
![[Pasted image 20251113174535.png]]
- Very low $\lambda$, under regularized model
	- Penalty term effect disappear
	- Loss depends only from data term
- we got the effect of
	- low bias
	- high variance
		- Test error can be high
		- possible overfitting
