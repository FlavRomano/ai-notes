The hyperplane $w^Tx_{i} + b$ 
- has equal distance to the 
	- closest positive
	- closest negative

![[Pasted image 20251104152909.png]]

We call **separation margin** $\rho$
- the double of the distance between
	- the hyperplane
	- the closest data point
- it's like a *safe zone*

$$\rho = \frac{2}{\lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert }$$
$$max\;\rho \iff min\;\lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert $$

---
Formally...

Considering 
- the [[202511041548|distance to the hyperplane]] $r$
- a positive [[202511041545|support vector]] $x^{(s)}$

$r$ for $x^{(s)}$ is $$\frac{g(x^{(s)})}{\lvert \lvert w_{o} \rvert  \rvert } = \frac{1}{\lvert \lvert w_{o} \rvert  \rvert } = \frac{\rho}{2}$$
> *remark*: it's obvious, considering that the margin $\rho$ is the **double** of the distance between $x^{(s)}$ and the hyperplane.

So we obtain the margin $$\rho= \frac{2}{\lvert \lvert w_{o} \rvert  \rvert }$$
We can conclude that the optimum hyperplane:
- **maximizes** the margin $\rho$ $$max \; \rho$$
- **minimizes** the length $\lvert \lvert w \rvert \rvert$ $$min\; \lvert \lvert w \rvert  \rvert $$
