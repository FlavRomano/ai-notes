The true error $R$ is limited from the $VC$-bound inequality $$R \le R_{emp} + \varepsilon \left( \frac{1}{l} , VC, \frac{1}{\delta} 
\right)$$ 
- $\varepsilon$ is a function that 
	- $VC-$penalty
	- grows with $VC$
	- decreases with higher $l$ and $\delta$
- $R_{emp}$
	- decreases using complex models
	- decreases with high $VC$
- $\delta$ is the confidence
	- it rules the probability that the bound holds
	- if you demand **higher confidence**, the penalty **grows**.
	- if you accept **lower confidence**, the penalty shrinks.

We want to stay in the middle, so minimize the bound  $$R_{emp} + \varepsilon \left( \frac{1}{l} , VC, \frac{1}{\delta} 
\right)$$ in order to minimize the risk and keep the complexity low. 

---

- It quantifies **how many samples are enough** to learn reliably.
    
- It explains the **bias–variance tradeoff** 
	- too simple implies high bias
	- too complex implies overfitting risk 
		- unless sample size is huge.
