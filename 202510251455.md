When we normalize a dataset, for each feature we obtain:
- zero mean $\mu = 0$
- standard deviation $\sigma=1$

---

> Saying “standard deviation = 1” means that the **average magnitude of deviations** from the mean is about 1 (in RMS terms)


The **standard deviation** (SD) of a dataset measures how spread out the values are around the **mean**.  
If we have data points $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_L$​ with mean $\mu$, then

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L} (x_i - \mu)^2}$$
When we say that **$σ = 1$**, we’re saying that the _average_ squared deviation from the mean is 1

>Conclusion: **on average**, the points are about one unit away from the mean.

---

By imposing mean $\mu=0$:
- The values are centered around zero (their positive and negative parts balance).
- The **average squared magnitude** of the points is 1.

So the standard deviation formula $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L} x_i^2}$$ becomes just the **root mean square (RMS)** of the data values themselves.

Now the dataset is _centered_ and _normalized_.
