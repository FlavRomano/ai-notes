A function $f:\mathbb{R}^D\to \mathbb{R}$, with a region $\mathbb{R}^D$, is called **probability density function** if 
1. $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^D \mid f(x) \ge 0$
2. it's integral exists  and $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^D} f(x) dx=1$$

So, probability density function is **any function $f$** that is 
- non-negative
- and integrates to one

We associate a random variable $X$ with this function $f$ by $$P(a\le X\le b) = \int_{a}^b f(x) dx, \quad a,b\in \mathbb{R},\quad x\in \mathbb{R}$$
- $a,b$ and $x$ are *outcomes* of the continuous random variable $X$
- $x\in \mathbb{R}^D$
- this association is called **law or distribution of the random variable $X$**

---

In contrast to discrete random variable
- the probability of a continuous random variable $X$ taking a particular value $P(X=x)$ is **zero**
- it's like to specify an interval on the integral where $a=b=x$
