Consider two random variables $X,Y$
- $X$ has 5 possible states
- $Y$ has 3 possible states

$$n_{ij} = \#\text{states with } X = x_{i} \text{ and } Y=y_{j}$$$$N=\#events$$
- The value $c_{i}$ is the sum of the individual frequencies for the $i$-th column $$c_{i} = \sum_{j=1}^3 n_{ij}$$
- The value $r_{j}$ is the sum of the individual frequencies for the $j$-th row $$r_{j} = \sum_{i=1}^5 n_{ij}$$
---

So the **marginal probability** can be seen as *the sum over a row or column*

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=x_{i}) &= \frac{c_{i}}{N} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^3 n_{ij}}{N} \\
P(Y=y_{j}) &= \frac{r_{j}}{N} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^5 n_{ij}}{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $c_{i}$ and $r_{j}$ are the $i$th column and $j$th row of the probability table:
![[Pasted image 20250628123444.png]]
For discrete random variables with a finite number of events **the probabilities sum up to one** $$\sum_{i=1}^5 P(X=x_{i}) = 1,\quad \sum_{j=1}^3 P(Y=y_{j}) = 1$$

---
The **conditional probability** is the *fraction of a row or column in a particular cell*.
- the conditional probability of $Y$ given $X$ is $$P(Y=y_{j} \mid X=x_{i}) = \frac{n_{ij}}{c_{i}}$$
- the conditional probability of $X$ given $Y$ is $$P(X=x_{i} \mid Y=y_{j}) = \frac{n_{ij}}{r_{j}}$$
