Q: Are probability theory and statistics the same?

E: 
- Using probability we can consider a **model** of some process
	- where the underlying uncertainty is captured by random variables
	- we use the rules of probability to derive what happens
- Using statistics we observe that something has happened and try to figure out the underlying process that explains the observations
- Machine Learning is close to statistics in its goal to construct a model that represents the process that generated the data.
	- using the rules of probability we can obtain a best-fitting model for some data

C: No, probability theory and statistics are not the same, though they are closely related and complementary fields.

Probability theory provides a framework for modeling and analyzing uncertainty. It starts with a known or assumed model of a process, often involving random variables, and uses its rules to predict the likelihood of different outcomes.

Statistics, on the other hand, works in the opposite direction. It begins with observed data and seeks to infer the underlying process or model that generated that data. Machine Learning is a field closely aligned with statistics in this regard, aiming to construct models from data that represent the generative process. While distinct, statistics often utilizes the tools and principles of probability theory to achieve its goals, for example, in finding the "best-fitting" model for observed data.
