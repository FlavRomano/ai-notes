In **batch gradient descent**
- you look at _every single training example_ 
- to calculate the slope before taking one step

So when we compute the $\nabla E(w)$, 
- for $l$ training examples 
- we want to minimize the sum of the individual losses for each example
- the derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, therefore
$$\frac{\partial E(w)}{\partial w_{j}} = -2 \sum_{p=1}^l (y_{p} - x_{p}^T w ) x _{p,j}$$
we have to sum over all $l$ training examples for every step.

n.b a step that covers all the training examples is called an **epoch**

---
Pros:
- Stable updates (smooth convergence).
- Makes full use of data (exact gradient).

Cons:
- Very slow if the dataset is huge.
- Requires a lot of memory (must process all data at once).
