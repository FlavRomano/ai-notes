> Inductive bias provides 
> - the _guiding assumptions_ that let a learning algorithm pick one plausible hypothesis 
> - and generalize to new data
> 	- instead of just memorizing the training set.

The induction problem tell us that:
- From the data alone, 
	- there are usually **many possible hypotheses** in $H$ 
	- that could explain the training examples. 
- Without making assumptions about the nature of the target function $f$
	- the model can’t know which $h$ to pick.

**Inductive bias** 
- is the set of assumptions a learning algorithm 
	- uses to prefer some hypotheses $h_k$ over others.
- it guides the algorithm’s search through the hypothesis space $H$.
- Without it generalization to unseen data would be impossible
	- the model would have no reason to favor $h_1$ over another $h_2$ that fits the training data equally well.

> No inductive bias means no generalization.
