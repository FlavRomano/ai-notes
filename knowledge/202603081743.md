The **Faithfulness Property** states that every conditional independence relation present in the true probability distribution must imply a d-separation in the graph $$Y_1 \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_2 \mid Z \implies Y_1 \perp\!\!\!\perp_{\mathcal{G}} Y_2 \mid Z$$

A **violation of faithfulness** occurs when 
- there is a statistical independence in the math/data (the probability distribution $\mathbb{P}$) 
- that is **not** reflected by a d-separation in the graph ($\mathcal{G}$) 

In other words, the graph tells you two variables are dependent (they have an unblocked path), but the actual mathematical distribution says they are independent.
