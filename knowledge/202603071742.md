This is a linear structure where influence flows in a single direction through a sequence of nodes.
*   $Y_1$ causes $Y_2$, and $Y_2$ causes $Y_3$.

According to the chain rule decomposition, the joint probability is factorized as:
$$P(Y_1, Y_2, Y_3) = P(Y_1) P(Y_2 \mid Y_1) P(Y_3 \mid Y_2)$$

The relationship between the start of the chain ($Y_1$) and the end of the chain ($Y_3$) depends entirely on the status of the intermediate node ($Y_2$).

*   $Y_2$ is Unobserved ![[Drawing 2026-03-07 17.49.57.excalidraw]]
    *   **Result:** $Y_1$ and $Y_3$ are **marginally dependent** $$Y_1 \not\perp Y_3$$
    * Information flows through the chain.
    * If you know something about the cause ($Y_1$), it changes your belief about the intermediate ($Y_2$), which in turn changes your belief about the effect ($Y_3$).

*   $Y_2$ is Observed ![[Drawing 2026-03-07 17.48.59.excalidraw]]
    *   **Result:** $Y_1$ and $Y_3$ become **conditionally independent** $$Y_1 \perp Y_3 \mid Y_2$$
    * $Y_2$ blocks the path from $Y_1$ to $Y_3$. The chain is broken by the observation. 
    *  Once you know the state of the intermediate link ($Y_2$), knowing the original cause ($Y_1$) gives you no *additional* information about the final effect ($Y_3$).
