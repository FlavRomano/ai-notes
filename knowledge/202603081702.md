A Bayesian Network is considered "faithful" if **every conditional independence relation in the actual probability distribution implies a d-separation in the graph.** 

$$Y_{1} \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_2 \mid Z \implies Y_{1} \perp\!\!\!\perp_{\mathcal{G}}  Y_{2} \mid Z$$

- left side
	- represents **conditional independence** in the probability distribution. 
- right side
	- represents **d-separation** in the graph $\mathcal{G}$.

If the distribution says $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ are independent given $Z$, then the graph *must* show them visually blocked (d-separated) by $Z$.

![[Excalidraw/Drawing 2026-03-08 16.33.26.excalidraw]]

- Is an easier assumption than global markov property 
	- because we require to represent all conditional independences.
- Is fundamental to **coincisely represent** joint distributions
	- the more conditional independences we represent
		- the less parameters we need to store in the model
	- typically we have more independence in the graph than to a distribution
