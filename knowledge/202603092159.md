When we apply the do-operator 
- we force a set of variables $V$ to take a specific constant value $k$
	- physically alter the model.
- break the incoming arrows to the variables
	- causal relation no longer holds

![[Drawing 2026-03-09 21.49.40.excalidraw]] 

In a bayesian network, the joint probability of all variables is calculated by multiplying the probability of each variable given its parents.
$$P(Y_{1}, Y_{2}, \dots, Y_{n}) = \prod_{i} P(Y_{i}\mid Pa(Y_{i}))$$

Truncated Factorization bridges the gap between joint probability and do-operator $$P(Y_{1}, Y_{2}, \dots, Y_{n}\mid do(V:=k)) = \prod_{Y_{i}\notin V} P(Y_{i} \mid Pa(Y_{i})) \cdot \prod_{Y_{j} \notin V} \mathbb{I}(Y_{j}=k_{j})$$

- every variable $Y_{i}$ not in $V$
	- continue to be influenced by their natural parents
- every intervened variable $Y_j$ in $V$
	- we truncate their original probability term $$P(Y_{j}\mid Pa(Y_{j}))$$
		- it no longer cares about its parents
	- we replace it with an indicator function $$\mathbb{I}(Y_{j}=k_{j})=\begin{cases}
1 &Y_{j}=k_{j} \\
0 &\text{o/w}
\end{cases}$$
