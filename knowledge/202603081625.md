A Bayesian Network respects the Global Markov condition if **every d-separation in the graph implies a valid conditional independence in the probability distribution.**

$$Y_{1} \perp\!\!\!\perp_{\mathcal{G}}  Y_{2} \mid Z \implies Y_{1} \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_2 \mid Z$$

- left part
	- if you look at the graph $\mathcal{G}$, the nodes $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ are visually blocked (d-separated) by the set of nodes $Z$.
- right part
	-  in the real-world data or the true joint probability distribution, the random variables $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ are statistically independent given $Z$.

We say that a bayesian network $\mathcal{G}_{Y}$ is markovian to a probability distribution $\mathbb{P}_{Y}$, it means that $\mathcal{G}_{Y}$  d-separation implies conditional independence.

![[Excalidraw/Drawing 2026-03-08 16.33.26.excalidraw]]

> The Global Markov Property is what makes Bayesian Networks so useful. It guarantees that we can just look at the arrows and nodes of the graph, apply the visual rules of d-separation (checking for forks, chains, and colliders), and confidently conclude that those variables are statistically independent.
