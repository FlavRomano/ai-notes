Having the input and weight vector$$x_{p}^T = (1,0,1) \quad w^T=(-0.2,0.5,-0.2)$$then the predicted output is $$x_{p}^T w = -0.2 + 0 - 0.2 = -0.4$$so the delta-p value $$\delta_{p} = y - x_{p}^Tw = 1 + 0.4 = 1.4$$and by the delta rule $$\Delta w_{j} = 2 \delta_{p} x_{p,j} = 2 \cdot 1.4 = 2.8$$
In order to compute eta, we exploit the example such that $$w_{1} = -0.2 \to 0.1\quad w_{3}=-0.2 \to 0.1$$
we know that it takes $$\Delta w_{j} = 0.3$$ to mach the new weights. So $$\begin{align}
\Delta w_{j} &= \eta (2 \cdot \delta_{p} \cdot x_{p,j}) \\
0.3 &=\eta(2 \cdot 1.4 \cdot 1)  \\
&\implies \eta= \frac{0.3}{2.8}=0.1
\end{align}$$
