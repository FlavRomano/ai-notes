Q: How the concept of association moved from Hume to associative memory?

E:
- all started with the definition of what an idea is
- phycologists think of ideas as nodes in a vast network called associative memory
	- each idea is linked to many others
	- there are different type of links
		- causes are linked to their effects (virus -> cold)
		- things to their properties (lime -> green)
		- things to their belonging categories (banana -> fruit)
- we have advanced beyond Hume
	- for Hume the mind hoes through a sequence of conscious ideas, one at a time
	- now we believe that one idea that gets activated activates many others
		- that activate others...
		- only few of this activated ideas will be registered in consciousness 

C: The understanding of association evolved from Hume's view of sequential, conscious ideas linked by resemblance, contiguity, and cause/effect, to modern psychology's concept of associative memory. This modern view sees ideas as nodes in a vast, interconnected network, where activating one idea automatically triggers many others, mostly unconsciously, representing a more complex and dynamic system of mental connections.
