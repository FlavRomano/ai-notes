We make a prediction considering **every possible hypothesis**

$$P(X\mid d) = \sum_{i} P(X \mid h_{i}) p(h_{i}\mid d)$$

- Calculates the prediction by taking a **weighted vote** of all possible hypotheses
- the weighted vote represent
	- how likely that hypothesis is to be true given the data observed so far (the Posterior)

Therefore the prediction is divided in two parts:
1. The probability of the new observation/event $X$, given that hypothesis $h_i$ is true. $$P(X \mid h_{i})$$ what each individual hypothesis predicts for the new data point $X$
2. What is the probability that hypothesis $h_{i}$ is actually the correct one? $$P(h_{i}\mid d)$$
