Q: What's associative activation?

E:
- It's a System 1 operation
- Yields a self-reinforcing patter of cognitive, emotional and physical responses
	- v. associatively coherent
- ideas that have been evoked trigger many other ideas
- each ideas is connected and each supports and strengthens the others.
	- word -> evokes memories
	- memories -> evoke emotions
	- emotions -> evoke facial expression
	- so the feeling intensify 
- even two simple words (like "banana vomit") can trigger this operation of System 1
	- System 1 made as much sense as possible of the situation
	- link the words together, in a causal story and creating a context for future development
		- so our brain is ready to recognize this patter all over again
		- it's prepared to this possibility
- System 1 treats this conjunction of two words as representations of reality
	- our body experiences an attenuated replica of a reaction to the real thing
	- the emotional response and physical recoil is part of the interpretation of the event


C: Associative activation is a core function of System 1, where the evocation of an idea triggers a cascade of interconnected cognitive, emotional, and physical responses. This self-reinforcing pattern is characterized by its associative coherence, meaning that each evoked idea strengthens and supports others, creating a consistent and often intense experience. For example, a word can trigger memories, which in turn evoke emotions, and these emotions can lead to physical expressions, intensifying the overall feeling. System 1 attempts to make sense of even simple conjunctions of ideas, like "banana vomit," by linking them into a causal story and preparing the brain to recognize similar patterns in the future. Crucially, System 1 treats these associations as representations of reality, leading to an attenuated, but real, physiological and emotional reaction, such as a physical recoil to an unpleasant thought.
