Thank to bayes rule, we know that the likelihood describe how data and hypothesis are related $$P(d \mid h)$$ the marginal likelihood is the quantity $$P(d) = \int P(d \mid h) P(h) \;dx= \mathbb{E}_{X}[P(d \mid h)]$$
- ensures the posterior is normalized
- can also be interpreted as the expected likelihood where we take the expectation with respect to the prior $P(h)$
