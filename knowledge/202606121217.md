The First-Order Markov Chains assumption tell us that **future depends only from present, not from the entire history**. 

Element $X_{t}$ only depends on its predecessor in the sequence.

![[Pasted image 20260612122110.png]]

Formally $$P(X_{t}\mid X_{1}, \dots, X_{t-1}) = P(X_{t}\mid X_{t-1})$$ this is a really strong assumption, because the joint probability can be factorize into $$P(X_{1}, \dots, X_{T}) = P(X_{1}) \prod_{t=2}^T P(X_{t} \mid X_{t-1})$$

- $P(X_{1})$ prior distribution
	- describe the probability of starting from a given state
- $P(X_{t} \mid X_{t-1})$ transition distribution
	- describe the probability of jumping from a state to the next one.

But it comes with a cost, this graphical model has a really bold assumption that **doesn't model complex relationship** between observed elements in a sequence.

![[Pasted image 20260612122805.png]]

Words dependencies is too complex, we can't just say $$P(is \mid cat)$$ because 
- "cat" appears in many distinct contexts
- observed words are just a superficial layer, consequences, for more deep structures
	- semantic
	- common knowledge
	- ...
