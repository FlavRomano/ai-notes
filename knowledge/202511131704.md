$$\begin{align}
E_{P}[(y - h(x))^2] &= E_{P}[h(x)^2 - 2yh(x) + y^2] \\
&= E_{P}[h(x)^2] + E_{P}[y^2] - 2E_{P}[y]E_{P}[h(x)]
\end{align}$$
We got three different terms.

## First term
Let $$\overline{h}(x) = E_{P}[h(x)]$$ denote the **mean prediction**
- of the hypothesis at $x$
- when $h$ is trained with data drawn by $P$

![[Pasted image 20251113172439.png]]

So using the [[202511131658|variance lemma]]$$E_{P}[h(x)^2] = E_{P}[(h(x) - \overline{h}(x))^2] + \overline h (x)^2$$
## Second term
Knowing that $$E_{P}[y] = E_{P}[f(x) + \varepsilon] = f(x)$$
![[Pasted image 20251113172521.png]]

using again the variance lemma we have $$E_{P}[y^2] = E_{P}[(y-f(x))^2] + f(x)^2$$

## Putting everything together
$$\begin{align}
E_{P}[(y - h(x))^2] &= E_{P}[h(x)^2 - 2yh(x) + y^2] \\
&= E_{P}[h(x)^2] + E_{P}[y^2] - 2E_{P}[y]E_{P}[h(x)] \\
&= \underbrace{E_{P}[(h(x) - \overline{h}(x))^2] + \overline h (x)^2}_{E_{P}[h(x)^2]} + \underbrace{E_{P}[(y-f(x))^2] + f(x)^2}_{E_{P}[y^2]}- 2E_{P}[y]E_{P}[h(x)] \\
&= \underbrace{E_{P}[(h(x)- \overline h(x))^2]}_{\text{variance}} + \underbrace{(\overline h(x) - f(x))^2}_{\text{bias}^2} + \underbrace{E_{P}[(y-f(x))^2]}_{\text{noise}^2} \\
&=Var[h(x)] + Bias[h(x)]^2 + E_{P}[\varepsilon^2] \\
&=Var[h(x)] + Bias[h(x)]^2 + \sigma^2
\end{align}$$
