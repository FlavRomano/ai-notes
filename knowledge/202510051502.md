$$h(x) = \begin{cases}
1 &x^Tw > 0.5 \\
0 &otherwise
\end{cases}$$
The decision boundary line defined by $$x^T w = 0.5$$ separates the input space in two region.
- red part
	- $1$
- green part
	- $0$

The $h(x)$ is the Linear threshold unit (activates when $x^Tw$ surpass 0.5).

- as you can see we are in underfitting
- the decision boundary is misclassifying a lot of training data points 

---

With 1-nn classifier
![[Pasted image 20251005153923.png]]
We have very flexible decision boundaries
- no misclassifications on training data
- the decision boundaries is **not linear**
	- and quite unstable
- it fits the noise!

---

With 15-nn classifier

The predicted class is chosen by majority vote amongst the 15-nearest neighbors

![[Pasted image 20251005161400.png]]
Increasing the $K$ in the $K$-nn algorithm provides a classification boundary that is
- smoother
	- lower variance than 1-nn
- generally more robust 

Although this, decision boundaries 
- are not linear 
- but less irregular than those produced by 1-nn
- they adapts to the **local densities of the classes**

Unlike 1-nn, we have some misclassifications in the training data.
- the majority vote can sometimes override the true label
- especially if that point is surrounded by members of the opposite class
