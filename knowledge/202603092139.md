An hard intervention is an **active manipulation** of the model.

This is denoted by the do-operator $$do(Y:=k)$$ setting the variable $Y$ to have constant value $k$.

In a causal bayesian network variables are **determined by their parents**, when we perform an hard intervention on a variable 
- we **replaces** the variable of the model with the constant value.
- that's cutting incoming arrows
	- $Y$ is no longer influenced by usual causes

![[Drawing 2026-03-09 21.49.40.excalidraw]] 

This is fundamentally the difference between conditioning and intervening $$P(Y_{2} \mid Y_{1} = k) \not= P(Y_{2} \mid do(Y_{1} :=k))$$
- conditioning (left member)
	- given the evidence $Y_1=k$, what's the probability of $Y_{2}$?
- intervening (right member)
	- forcing $Y_1=k$, what's the probability of $Y_{2}$?

That's the reason behind truncated factorization.
