K-nearest Neighbors use the most natural way to classify a new point
- it looks at its neighbors
- take an average $$avg_{k}(x)= \frac{1}{k} \sum_{x\in N_{k}(x)} y_{i}$$
	- $N_{k}(x)$ is a neighborhood of $x$ that contains exactly $k$ neighbors

If there is a clear dominance of one of the classes in the neighborhood of $x$
- then it's likely that the observation $x$ itself would belong to that class too
- **majority voting** classification rule
	- among the members of $N_{k}(x)$ $$h(x) = \begin{cases}
1 &x^Tw > 0.5 \\
0 &otherwise
\end{cases} \quad \text{ for targets }y\text{ in } \{0,1\}$$

The selection of $K$ (e.g., $K=15$) is crucial
- too high $K$
	- _underfitting_ 
- too low $K$
	- _overfitting_

For multi-class
- k-nn return the class most common
- amongst its $k$ nearest neighbors $$h(x) = {\text{arg max}}_{v} \sum_{x_{i}\in N_{k}(x)}1_{v,y_{i}}$$ $$1_{v,y_{i}} = \begin{cases}
1 &v=y_{i} \\
0 &otherwise
\end{cases}$$ we count the classes in the neighbor, taking the most frequent class (arg max)
