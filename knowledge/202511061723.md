We can truly say that solving the problem in the feature space is [[202511061712|not a feasible solution]]. Because we can't evaluate $\Phi(x)$.

In fact under certain conditions 
- we don't need to evaluate directly $\Phi(x)$
- we don't need to know the feature space itself

This is possible using the [[202511061726|inner product kernel function]] $k$. The dot product in the feature space
- is evaluated without considering
	- the feature mapping
	- and the feature space itself
- is evaluated in terms of the input patterns.

We can arrange the dot products in the feature space between the images of the TR patterns in a $N\times N$ matrix called **kernel matrix**.
$$
K =
\begin{pmatrix}
k({x}_1, {x}_1) & \cdots & k({x}_1, {x}_N) \\
k({x}_2, {x}_1) & \cdots & k({x}_2, {x}_N) \\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
k({x}_N, {x}_1) & \cdots & k({x}_N, {x}_N)
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$K$ is symmetrical because the inner product kernel is symmetrical.

From the [[202511061754|primal problem]] we found the [[202511061759|dual problem]] and then we solve that.
