Lastly in the likelihood we introduce indicator variables $\overline z_{jm}$  that are one-hot-encoding of $z_{j}$ $$\overline z_{jm} = \begin{cases}
1 &z_{j} = m \\
0 &\text{o/w}
\end{cases}$$

so we can rewrite the likelihood as $$\prod_{m}[\pi_{m}P(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \theta)]^{\overline z_{jm}}$$ and after taking the log likelihood we get a much easier version to differentiate $$\prod_{m}[\pi_{m}P(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \theta)]^{\overline z_{jm}} = \sum_{m} \overline z_{jm} \log[\pi_{m} P(x_{j} \mid \mu_{m}, \sigma_{m})]$$
