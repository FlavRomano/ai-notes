When we say that observations $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ are IID we are saying that the observations are
- **independent**
	- they do not influence each other $$P(x_{i}\mid x_{j}, \theta) = P(x_{i}\mid \theta)\quad \forall i\neq j$$
- **identically distributed**
	- every observation comes from the same probability distribution $$x_{i} \sim P(x\mid \theta) \quad \forall i$$
	- all samples are generated using the same parameter $\theta$

It's very important in Maximum Likelihood Learning because it allows us to write $$P(x_{1}, \dots, x_{N}\mid \theta) = \prod_{i=1}^N P(x_{i}\mid \theta)$$ making the likelihood tractable.
