The multivariate Gaussian plays a central role in data analysis.

For a k-dimensional probabilistic vector $$\vec{x} = (x_{1}, \dots,x_{k})^T, x_{i}\in \mathbb{R}[0,1]$$

$$p(\vec{x}\mid \vec{\mu}, \Sigma) = N(\vec{x}\mid \vec{\mu}, \Sigma) \equiv \frac{1}{\sqrt{ \det(2\pi \Sigma) }} \exp\left( - \frac{1}{2} (\vec{x}-\vec{\mu})^T  \Sigma^{-1} (\vec{x}-\vec{\mu}) \right)$$

- $\vec{\mu}$ is the **mean vector** of the distribution $$\vec{\mu} = \mathbb{E}[\vec{x}] = (\mathbb{E}[x_{1}], \dots, \mathbb{E}[x_{k}])^T$$
- $\Sigma$ is the **covariance matrix** $$\Sigma  = \mathbb{E}[(\vec{x} - \vec{\mu})(\vec{x} - \vec{\mu})^T]$$
	- its inverse $\Sigma^{-1}$ is called **precision**
