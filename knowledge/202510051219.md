As the number of dimensions (features) increases:

- The **volume of the space** grows **exponentially**.  
- Data points become **sparse** (far apart from each other).
- The concept of **distance and similarity** becomes less meaningful.
	- breaks algorithms that rely on distance (e.g knn)
- Models need **much more data** to learn reliably.
	- In 1D, a few dozen points may cover the space well.
	- In 100D, you might need millions of points.

So, even though high-dimensional features might seem to give more information, they often make learning and generalization **harder**, not easier.

---

Imagine points distributed uniformly in a 1D interval $[0,1]$:

- If you want to cover 10% of the space, you only need 0.1 of the interval.

In 2D, to cover 10% of the area of a unit square, you need a square of side:

$$\sqrt{0.1} \approx 0.32$$

In 10D, to cover 10% of the hypercube’s volume, you need a hypercube of side:

$$0.1^{1/10} \approx 0.79$$
