The goal of Maximum Likelihood Learning is estimate the parameter $\theta$ that best explains the observed data. 

In another way, find the model $\theta$ most likely to have generated the dataset.

> MLE assumes that observation are $x_{1},\dots, x_{n}$ **independent and identically distributed**

Once data are observed we consider a likelihood function $$\mathcal{L} (\theta\mid \mathcal{D}) = P(\mathcal{D} \mid \theta)$$ 

We want to estimate the parameter by maximizing the likelihood $$\begin{align}
\theta_{ML} &= \arg \max_{\theta} P(\mathcal{D} \mid \theta) \\
&=\arg \max_{\theta} \prod_{i=1}^N P(x_{i} \mid \theta)
\end{align}$$

We got a bunch of products, we don't like them because
- they are numerically unstable
- they are difficult to differentiate

We can use the logarithm, because is strictly increasing $$\arg \max f = \arg \max \log(f)$$

Therefore we define the log-likelihood $$\begin{align}
\mathcal{l}(\theta)&= \log\left[ \prod_{i=1}^N P(x_{i} \mid \theta) \right] \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^N \log P(x_{i}\mid \theta)
\end{align}$$

MLE solves $$\theta_{ML} = \arg \max_{\theta} \mathcal{l}(\theta)$$ checking that the solution is a maximum $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{l(\theta)}}{\partial  \theta} = 0$$
