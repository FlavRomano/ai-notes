Visually, this structure consists of a central node ($Y_2$) that acts as a parent to two other nodes ($Y_1$ and $Y_3$)

![[Drawing 2026-03-07 17.31.17.excalidraw]]


According to the Bayesian Network factorization rules, the joint probability for this structure is:
$$P(Y_1, Y_3, Y_2) = P(Y_1 \mid Y_2) P(Y_3 \mid Y_2) P(Y_2)$$

The relationship between the children ($Y_1$ and $Y_3$) depends entirely on whether the common cause ($Y_2$) is observed or not.
- $Y_{2}$ unobserved ![[Drawing 2026-03-07 17.38.50.excalidraw]]
	- **Result:** $Y_1$ and $Y_3$ are **marginally dependent** $$Y_1 \not\perp Y_3$$
	- Information "flows" through the unobserved node.
	- If you don't know the cause ($Y_2$) knowing one effect ($Y_1$) gives you information about the likely state of the cause, which in turn changes your probability for the other effect ($Y_3$). 
- $Y_{2}$ observed ![[Drawing 2026-03-07 17.39.53.excalidraw]]
	- **Result:** $Y_1$ and $Y_3$ become **conditionally independent** $$Y_1 \perp Y_3 \mid Y_2$$
	- Observing $Y_2$ **blocks the path** from $Y_1$ to $Y_3$.
	- If you already know the cause ($Y_2$), knowing one effect ($Y_1$) gives you no *new* information about the other effect ($Y_3$). The dependence is broken.
