Bayes rule connect likelihood, prior and posterior $$P(h\mid d) = \frac{P(d\mid h) p(h)}{p(d)},\quad P(d) = \sum_{h'\in H} P(d \mid h')p(h')$$ $d$ are observable data.

Most of the times we interpret hypothesis $h$ as an unknown parameter $\theta$. That said, the probability $$P(\theta \mid d)$$ represents the value of my unknown parameter after observing data, AKA **posterior**

The likelihood of $h$ is $$P(d \mid h)$$ describes how data and hypothesis are related.
