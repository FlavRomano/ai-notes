Obtaining the complete likelihood through marginalization over latent variable $z_{j}$

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta \mid X) = \prod_{j=1}^N \sum_{m=1}^M P(x_{j}, z_{j} = m \mid \theta)$$

We can do a step further, using the product rule $$P(x_{j}, z_{j}=m \mid \theta) = P(z_{j} = m \mid \theta)P(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \theta)$$

and knowing that $\theta = (\pi, \mu, \sigma)$ 

$$\begin{align}
P(x_{j}, z_{j}=m \mid \theta) &= P(z_{j} = m \mid \theta)P(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \theta) \\
&= \underbrace{P(z_{j} = m \mid \pi)}_{\text{categorical}} \underbrace{P(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \mu, \sigma)}_{\text{univariate gaussian}}
\end{align}$$

1. $P(z_{j} = m \mid \pi) = \pi_{m}$
	- is categorical
	- $z_{j}$ choose which component $m$ generated the observation
2. $(x_{j}\mid z_{j} = m, \mu, \sigma) = \mathcal{N}(x_{j}\mid \mu_{m}, \sigma_{m})$
	- is a univariate gaussian
	- after choosing $z_{j} = m$ we fixed the distribution that generated the observation
	- so the $m$ gaussian generates $x_j$

So each component contributes with the probability of being chosen (1)  and probability of generating $x_j$ (2)
